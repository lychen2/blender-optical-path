# Compact floating optical apparatus

Use this brief for a compact, publication-style apparatus overview. It preserves the complete source-to-detector topology while emphasizing optical elements and beam behavior. It is not a dimensional assembly drawing.

## What makes the reference readable

- The apparatus forms one compact horizontal group. The input optics, two short parallel arms and detector read as one continuous system; the gap between the arms is only large enough to separate them visually.
- Scale follows explanatory importance. Sources are small, lenses and mirrors have clear silhouettes, splitter cubes mark junctions, and recognizable objectives and a camera anchor the right half. Large finned laser housings do not dominate the diagram.
- Saturated beam bodies carry the route. Pale cyan glass contrasts with the purple combined-channel path and neutral housings. The beam changes width at optics instead of remaining an undifferentiated tube.
- A shallow oblique view exposes the optical faces and separates the branches without spreading them across a deep, empty scene. A light cool background and restrained shadows give depth without competing with the path.
- Short dark labels sit close to the corresponding optics, above or below each branch. Nearby labels rarely need leader lines. The image-plane marker and axial dimension occupy their own small region near the detector.

These are visual observations, not evidence for focal lengths, coating spectra or the reference's physical scale. Preserve supplied optical constraints when adapting this composition.

## Reusable prompt

> Create an editable, compact 3D optical apparatus figure showing **[source-to-detector topology]**. Use the component library as a starting point and create scene-local display copies.
>
> Compose the full apparatus as one tightly grouped horizontal system. Use a shallow oblique orthographic or weak-perspective view that reveals the optical faces. Keep parallel branches close but visibly separate, shorten passive free-space gaps, and allocate enough room at splitters and crossing paths to make the connections unambiguous. Fit the apparatus and its labels closely into the canvas; avoid large empty margins. Choose the aspect ratio from the topology, not from a fixed render preset.
>
> Scale optical symbols for readability, independently of their commercial diameter. Enlarge small lenses, mirrors and splitter cubes; reduce overlarge source housings. Preserve each component's recognizable proportions and place its optical center on the beam after resizing. Record display scales separately from physical dimensions. Do not stretch the whole scene or distort optical surfaces to achieve compactness.
>
> Make the beam a thick, saturated, clearly visible body with restrained transparency and no decorative fog or bloom. Keep single-wavelength colors and combined-channel colors consistent. Show expansion, recollimation, convergence, a finite waist and subsequent divergence where the optical train calls for them. Use wavelength-dependent grating directions only when a grating is present; follow the input spectrum and diffraction-order convention. Do not add an unsupported rainbow. Use known optical parameters when available; otherwise identify the envelope as schematic without inventing specifications. The central axis can remain straight through an on-axis lens; the envelope boundaries carry the focusing behavior.
>
> Use pale cyan optical glass with enough edge contrast to remain legible at final size, neutral satin objective/camera housings, a light cool gray background and soft restrained shadows. Avoid excessive hardware detailing, mirror-like glare, milky transparent optics, washed-out beams and large floor shadows. Review at the intended publication width before delivery.

## Negative prompt

> No breadboard or mounting hardware in floating mode; no oversized finned source housings; no widely spaced optical train; no tiny nearly invisible lenses; no unchanged constant-width tube through all focusing optics; no thin single-line beams unless explicitly requested; no forced rainbow; no giant empty floor; no extreme top-down view; no decorative glow; no long crossing leaders; no white-outlined labels; no manufacturer lettering; no text rendered inside the 3D scene.

## Final-size review

For a wide apparatus, first inspect at roughly 700–900 pixels across, then at the actual target size. This is a review scale, not a universal image specification.

1. Can the full source-to-detector route be followed without zooming? Are branch junctions clear?
2. Are small optics identifiable, and are sources subordinate to the optical train? Check resizing has not moved apertures off the beam.
3. Do the envelope boundaries visibly show the intended optical changes without implying unsupported measurements?
4. Does the system occupy the canvas, with only the space needed for optical separation and labels?
5. Are labels readable and close to their targets, and are glass edges visible against the background? For splitters, can the cube, its film and the internal beam junction all be identified? For flat shading, are visible boundaries complete, including nanostructures, substrate corners, objective end faces and barrel steps? Check edge coverage first, then stroke width. Keep beams free of outlines.

For a floating/mechanical comparison, preserve topology, terminology and channel colors. Floating mode may use tighter spacing and enlarged symbols; mechanical mode retains native dimensions and checks physical interfaces. State the differing display scale so the comparison is not read as a dimensional overlay.
